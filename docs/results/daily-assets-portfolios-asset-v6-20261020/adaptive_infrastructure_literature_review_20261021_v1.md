# Adaptive forecasting infrastructure: literature opportunity map

**Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 2026-10-21 · **Status:** research-only

## Scope and evidence boundary

This review compares selection and validation infrastructure around the existing fixed 167-recipe menu. It proposes no new numerical recipe and changes no source or production behavior. The working design produces joint asset forecasts, samples and accounts for a complete portfolio path, validates at historical origins, and learns a request-specific mixture from an endpoint-CRPS quadratic. The canonical external score is a lead-weighted marginal CRPS over cumulative portfolio-return distributions, using training-SD times square-root-lead normalization and normalized log-calendar lead weights. Those marginals come from coherent joint asset paths with within-path accounting; the score does not directly evaluate the complete path's cross-horizon or cross-asset dependence. Path-dependence diagnostics have zero score weight. The reported 14.61% gain is on 355 reused development requests; it is a promising development result, not independent, broad, or full-panel evidence.

The local selector record documents a different, earlier capped-origin prototype that lost on its matched 355 cases and an all-history selector with diagnostic-only evidence. Those receipts should not be merged with the current snapshot.

Already-tested dimensions are excluded here: endpoint versus path objective, selecting versus stacking, doubling versus half-doubling origins, 2/4/8/30 internal paths, and nested versus flat organization.

## Validity and path constraints

- Each training row is a historical portfolio request at an origin. Fit histories, features, model predictions, and loss calculations must use only information available then. The target horizon must be the requested horizon; shorter horizons can be separate prior information but cannot stand in as direct validation of a longer horizon.
- A mixture/gate selects among complete joint asset-path generators. Sample one component for each scenario path, then retain that component's joint asset dependence and perform holdings/rebalancing/accounting within the path. Do not choose component weights independently by asset or forecast day unless a separate joint dependence construction is validated.
- Fit meta-weights against the currently approved inner objective, but use the unchanged canonical lead-weighted marginal CRPS over cumulative portfolio-return distributions on matched outer requests for advancement. Forecast marginals must still come from coherent joint asset paths and within-path accounting. Calibration, complete output, data freshness, and reliability remain guardrails; they are not replacement selection objectives.
- Paired uncertainty should be clustered by forecast-origin time blocks, keeping all assets in a block together. Resample the canonical marginal-score reduction rather than treating 1,264 assets or overlapping windows as iid. A non-overlap estimate is useful as a conservative sensitivity check, not as a reason to change the target horizon. The canonical score and its diagnostics do not directly assess the full cross-horizon or cross-asset copula.
- The MCRPS literature learns marginal weights across quantiles and coordinates while retaining a copula. It does not establish that coordinate-wise reweighting preserves Simfolio's multi-asset path law.

## Finite stopping rule for an infrastructure phase

Freeze the recipe menu, score, origin mapping, feature definitions, and candidate contrasts before evaluation. Use one training-only tuning run per method family, then one locked paired comparison on a predeclared reserved time block with matched requested horizons and final path count; record prior block reuse and do not call it independent if it has been inspected before. Advance only methods that improve the canonical external marginal score without integrity or path/accounting failures; send at most those survivors to a broader matched panel, and run a complete panel only for a survivor. If none survives the reserved/broad checks after these predeclared contrasts, close this infrastructure phase with no promotion and report the result. Because development requests have already been reused, describe the evidence as exploratory and do not attach an independence or significance claim to the 355-request gain.

## Sources

1. [Berrisch & Ziel (2021), *CRPS Learning*](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.00968) — full paper: quantile aggregation, batch/online learning, BOA, smoothing, forgetting, and shrinkage.
2. [Berrisch & Ziel (2024), *Multivariate Probabilistic CRPS Learning with an Application to Day-Ahead Electricity Prices*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10019) — full accepted preprint: smoothing across quantile and marginal dimensions; explicitly leaves the copula unchanged.
3. [Yao et al. (2021), *Bayesian hierarchical stacking: Some models are (somewhere) useful*](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.08954) — input-dependent weights with partial pooling; explains overfit risk under no-pooling and extends to time series.
4. [Tashman (2000), *Out-of-sample tests of forecasting accuracy: an analysis and review*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(00)00065-0) — rolling origins, multiple test periods, recalibration, and evaluation design.
5. [Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, §5.10](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html) — rolling forecasting origin and multi-step errors at a specified horizon.
6. [West (1996), *Asymptotic Inference about Predictive Ability*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2171956) — inference from time-ordered out-of-sample predictions and prediction errors, including dependence.
7. [Diebold & Mariano (1995), *Comparing Predictive Accuracy*](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599) — loss-differential comparisons that allow serially correlated, non-Gaussian forecast errors.
8. [Smith & Wallis (2009), *A Simple Explanation of the Forecast Combination Puzzle*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0084.2008.00541.x) — finite-sample weight estimation error; point-forecast/MSFE evidence used only to motivate, not predict, the CRPS result.
9. [Flaspohler et al. (2021), *Online Learning with Optimism and Delay*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/flaspohler21a.html) — online updates with delayed labels and regret guarantees.
10. [Giacomini & White (2006), *Tests of Conditional Predictive Ability*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00718.x) — conditional versus unconditional forecast comparison when model performance may vary with information.
11. [Akiba et al. (2019), *Optuna: A Next-generation Hyperparameter Optimization Framework*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10902) — search/pruning infrastructure; it optimizes user-supplied objectives and does not generate forecasts.
