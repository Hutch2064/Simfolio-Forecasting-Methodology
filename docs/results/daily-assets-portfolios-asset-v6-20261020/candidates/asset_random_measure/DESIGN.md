# A random joint empirical law instead of a separate Gaussian drift draw

Each scenario draws a complete vector q of historical restart probabilities. All constituent assets share q and every historical-row index. Conditional on q, source rows follow a circular stationary-bootstrap chain: continue to the next historical row or restart from q, with average length L estimated from training returns and squared returns by the previously qualified corrected Politis–White implementation. Initialize from that chain's stationary distribution conditional on q. There is no fixed model mixture, hand-set decay cutoff or portfolio-level forecasting shortcut.

Two frozen concentration estimators are tested. The Rubin contrast uses Dirichlet(1,...,1) restart probabilities, total concentration n. The other estimates a single total concentration from training mean uncertainty. Let B_i=(1-r)/(1-r^n) sum(k=0..n-1) r^k X_(i+k mod n), r=1-1/L. The conditional stationary mean under q equals q'B. If q has equal Dirichlet parameters totaling A, covariance(q'B)=population_covariance(B)/(A+1). Compute the exact finite-n stationary-bootstrap mean covariance C from the earlier qualified Markov/FFT calculation. Fit A by matching standardized marginal trace: A=max(0,trace(cov(B)/daily_variance)/trace(C/daily_variance)-1). This matches a trace, not the entire covariance matrix. A=0 is the constrained maximum-dispersion limit, implemented as a categorical one-hot q draw, rather than an arbitrary positive floor.

The induced covariance of q'B determines the existing smooth mean shrinkage toward zero. There is no additional Gaussian population-mean draw: random q already introduces joint uncertainty in the mean, variance, tails and dependence of the empirical law. Three raw volatility treatments are none, training-fitted GARCH fade and training-fitted EWMA fade. Those crossed with the two concentration rules yield six single generative procedures. Fading scales centered historical returns and returns toward historical variance; all mean treatment is documented and training-only. No conditioning on class, ticker, horizon or held-out error is used.

Rubin (1981), The Bayesian Bootstrap, https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176345338 , motivates random probability measures on empirical observations. This time-series Markov extension and concentration estimator are our working resampling model, not Rubin's exact IID posterior and not a guaranteed Bayesian posterior for dependent returns. A related distinction is discussed by the authors of Estimator Statistics from Simulation-Free Dirichlet Block-Bootstrap Resampling, https://doi.org/10.3390/stats9020032 . Neither paper establishes that this forecast model beats the reference. The mechanism differs from the previous empirical bootstrap-mean offset experiment: here one random measure drives every joint future observation and all its moments, replacing the standalone drift draw.

Qualification compares conditional means with explicit circular sums, stationary probabilities with a Markov solve, Dirichlet-induced mean covariance with simulation, chunk invariance, exact finite path counts and joint asset accounting. The current working incumbent and fixed WR reference are replayed at matching simulation counts. The panel, overall score and stopping conditions remain unchanged. Screens are exploratory and adaptively reused; validation uses1000paths, without claims of independence or significance. No automatic full-panel evaluation follows a small gain.
