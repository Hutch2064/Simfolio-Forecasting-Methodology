# Training-fitted Student innovation pool prototype

Current leader uses a learned training-CRPS pool of EWMA-fading joint raw blocks and SV-RMS Gaussian shocks. Earlier Student fixed-half feature variants lost the72-case screen; empirical RMS innovations improved development but lost reserved/broad. This does not establish a Student improvement. The new mechanism is to fit the blend against its actual unit-variance Student distribution rather than reuse Gaussian weights or a fixed half split.

Preserve asset-level joint paths, raw blocks/EWMA fading, fitted SV-RMS scale, mean shrinkage, correlated mean uncertainty and portfolio accounting. Student df is fitted on training filtered innovations using the existing parent estimator; a common radial draw preserves joint elliptical dependence. The Gaussian endpoint is retained when inverse df is exactly zero. Mixture weights are estimated on the same marginal training distributional objective, with exact leave-one-out empirical raw terms and analytic Student absolute-distance/self-distance terms. Raw/Student cross-distance uses convex chord interpolation with a distribution-derived relative1e-6 numerical error bound, matching the incumbent numerical precision. There are no new horizon/ticker/class rules, financial cutoffs or outer-score fitted weights.

Require independent CDF-integral CRPS verification, brute-force small-pool leave-one-out mixture coefficients, the derived interpolation bound, exact Gaussian endpoint controls at100 and1000paths, exact simulation count/chunk accounting and history identity before field admission. No field result or improvement is claimed by this prototype.

Student CRPS reference: Jordan, Krueger and Lerch (2019), https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v090i12 and the official scoringRules Student distribution documentation. For unit-variance Student T, E|x-sT| follows its CDF/PDF expression; half E|T-T'| is2sqrt(nu-2)/(nu-1) B(1/2,nu-1/2)/B(1/2,nu/2)^2. For g(s)=E|X-sT|, g''(s)<=K/s with K=2 max_u u^2 f_T(u) and g(s)>=sE|T|. Thus a linear chord's relative error is bounded by K(r-1)^2/(8E|T|), establishing the numerical grid from tolerance rather than an arbitrary model parameter.
