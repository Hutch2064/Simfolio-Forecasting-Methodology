# Empirical joint-return regimes with data-derived fading

This new single generative model fits a global latent volatility regime to the joint asset training history. Standardize each constituent by its full-prefix mean and standard deviation, then use log(mean of squared standardized constituent returns+1/n) as a shared signal. A two-state Gaussian hidden Markov model has fitted state means, a fitted common emission variance, fitted transitions and fitted initial probabilities. The common variance avoids the isolated-component variance-collapse pathology of freely fitted separate variances. The signal is a summary approximation, not an exact likelihood for all asset returns. All parameters use only the available training prefix.

Numerical initial states come from the observed signal median. The two optimizer starts use observed adjacent-label transition counts or their observed independent frequencies; these are numerical starts, not fixed forecast percentages. Analytic-gradient likelihood optimization checks success and per-observation gradient. Bounds at35in log-odds and machine-epsilon common variance are numerical resolution guards, not financial volatility floors or horizon decay choices. Truly constant signals have an explicit one-state boundary. Failed fits are reported; there is no replacement model or synthetic score penalty.

For each historical row, obtain smoothed training state probabilities. These determine the within-state empirical source-row probabilities. During simulation, start from the final filtered state posterior, transition once per future observation, and sample one complete joint asset-return row from the sampled state's historical distribution. Consequently current regime conditioning decays according to the fitted transition matrix rather than a hard-coded horizon cutoff or mixture percentage. Empirical conditional emissions preserve joint tail observations; the model is not a Gaussian asset-return model. There is no raw/FHS half-and-half allocation or asset-class/horizon routing.

Four frozen contrasts cross forced two-state versus training BIC selection of one/two states with historical state-conditional mean versus a common historical mean. The common-mean option recenters each state's historical pool to the same historical mean. Both keep the prior data-derived smooth mean shrinkage and correlated Gaussian mean-uncertainty draw. BIC uses parameter counts2for the one-state Gaussian signal and6for the two-state common-variance HMM, including one free initial probability. Counting state numbers is a declared model family, not an outer-score-selected combination. No state-count, prior, split, seed or duration retuning follows observed panel scores.

The older portfolio-level Gaussian-HMM contrast lost development. That is preserved in the research repository at docs/results/daily-cross-asset-722/research/Gaussian-HMM-volatility-20261020/decision.json. This new contrast differs materially: joint asset-level simulation, empirical joint return emissions instead of Gaussian asset returns, learned state locations on a cross-asset volatility signal, no fixed raw/model mixture, and optional training-selected null state. The earlier failure does not establish this mechanism works; it warrants only a cheap controlled screen.

Primary motivation: Rydén, Teräsvirta and Åsbrink (1998), Stylized facts of daily return series and the hidden Markov model, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/%28SICI%291099-1255%28199805/06%2913%3A3%3C217%3A%3AAID-JAE476%3E3.0.CO%3B2-V ; Hamilton's regime work, https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jhamilto/pub_metrics . The empirical emission adaptation and common-signal approximation are ours, not an exact replication or an established forecast improvement.
