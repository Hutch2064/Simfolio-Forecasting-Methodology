# Training-selected joint blocks: no fixed component mixture

These fourteen frozen exploratory laws are seven single generative mechanisms crossed with two training-only estimates of uncertainty in the population mean. There is no percentage allocation between two forecast models and no asset, date, family or horizon routing. The unchanged panel, references and canonical score are external to the fitted model.

For each training prefix, estimate the stationary-bootstrap average length on each centered return and centered squared return (standardized residuals for a filtered law), using `arch` 8.0.0's corrected Politis–White selector. Use the largest marginal estimate so a shared joint source-row stream retains the longest detected marginal dependence. This maximum is an explicit aggregation convention, not a uniquely optimal multivariate estimator. A block has geometric length and a circular historical start; every asset receives the same row index. Length cannot be below one. A constant square feature has no identifiable dependence and contributes length one. All other invalid estimates fail visibly. The selector is optimal for its bootstrap variance-estimation criterion, not guaranteed best for forecasting.

References: https://bashtage.github.io/arch/bootstrap/generated/arch.bootstrap.optimal_block_length.html ; Politis and White (2004), https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf ; corrected by Patton, Politis and White (2009), DOI 10.1080/07474930802459016.

Single laws: raw blocks with no volatility overlay; raw blocks with GARCH persistence fade; raw blocks with likelihood-fitted EWMA fade; block-resampled GARCH standardized residuals; expected-variance EWMA residuals; conditional RMS log-OU residuals; and simulated log-OU residuals. Volatility estimators are the frozen earlier research fitters. Source residuals are jointly resampled as blocks rather than independent rows. The RMS and path log-OU fits remain moment-based approximations. Dependent innovations can change a recursive filter's unconditional variance: do not claim an exact variance-target identity under blocked residuals. These are controlled generative candidates, not faithful replicas of older panel winners.

The mean point estimate uses training-derived smooth shrinkage toward zero. One contrast retains the earlier weighted-batch Gaussian mean uncertainty. The other uses the exact conditional covariance of a length-n stationary-bootstrap sample mean, approximated by a joint Gaussian draw. For centered X with n rows and continuation probability r=1-1/L, let Gamma[k]=X' circular_shift(X,-k)/n. Then covariance(mean*) = {n Gamma[0] + sum(k=1..n-1) (n-k) r^k (Gamma[k]+Gamma[k]')}/n^2. This follows directly from the stationary index Markov transition, and an FFT evaluates the identical finite sum. The Gaussian interpretation as population-drift uncertainty is a modeling approximation, not an exact Bayesian posterior. Its scale is not adjusted from held-out scores. Tests compare FFT covariance with an explicit Markov calculation and simulated bootstrap means.

All paths use joint asset shocks, correlated mean draws and unchanged in-path portfolio accounting. No clipping, discarded paths or synthetic failure penalties. Training fits and dependence estimates are shared between the two mean-uncertainty contrasts. First screen uses 100 paths on the frozen 72-case/70-family sample, compared with exactly matched 100-path incumbent and fixed-reference forecasts. Promising laws require 1,000-path development and reserved checks; broad checks only when informative. No automatic full-panel escalation: the unchanged goal's stopping review precedes the final retained full evaluation.
