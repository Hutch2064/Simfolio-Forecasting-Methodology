# Fading latent mean: frozen adapter specification

This adapter supersedes the fit-only admission status described in the historical PROTOTYPE.md record. It adds a deterministic conditional mean curve to the current training-CRPS-weighted EWMA/raw and conditional-RMS Gaussian working model. It applies the offset inside every constituent asset step of both branches before portfolio accounting. The fitted pool fraction, innovation paths, volatility, shrinkage and correlated population-mean uncertainty remain unchanged.

For each asset, fit a stationary latent AR(1) mean to centered standardized training returns. The state persistence and signal share are likelihood-estimated; no outer loss, hand-set decay duration, horizon gate, training split or asset-class route enters. Two noise contrasts use constant conditional variance or the parent model's training conditional variance, the latter computed from its training-fitted raw fraction and both component scales. Two selection contrasts retain the fitted likelihood improvement or choose the zero-state null by training BIC. Their cross yields four declared global model-fitting rules. Model selection on the outer panel always uses only the unchanged overall canonical Q.

Future point offsets equal training standard deviation times final filtered state mean times fitted persistence^lead. The point curve naturally decays, and may alternate if training estimates negative persistence. It is identical across scenarios conditional on the fitted prefix. The existing joint mean-uncertainty draw is retained. This is a semiparametric mean-conditioning contrast, not the exact full predictive covariance or stochastic latent-state simulation of the fitted state-space model. A full stochastic mean-path or mean-adjusted-innovation refit would be a distinct later test, not silently added here.

Three fit tests verify gradients, the null, causal state timing and synthetic recovery. Four adapter tests verify exact byte-for-byte zero-curve parent replay at100and1000paths, joint accounting, exact counts, chunk invariance, prefix identity and the single-asset cumulative point shift.
