# Independent strict-FAST stopping review

**Date:** 2026-10-19  
**Scope:** bounded review of frozen source and saved diagnostics. No forecasts, model fits, timing runs, builds, or network calls. This note supplements, and does not replace, the dated stopping assessment.

## Finding

The parent assessment is appropriately bounded: the reviewed evidence supports **no currently evidence-backed new scoring interaction that is also a plausible strict-FAST candidate**. It does not establish that no such law exists. I found no basis to weaken strict FAST to M261, combine unpaired medians, or declare the research goal complete. The canonical panel comparison, publication/archival checks, and final recommendation remain outstanding.

## What the saved evidence does establish

The exact native-bound cDCC optimization is a same-law implementation change, not a new financial candidate. Its 27 trials cover 162 forecast invocations and show selected-FAST time ratios of 1.006/0.987 fresh/cached for SPY 8,766; 0.975/0.885 for six assets at 126; and 0.929/0.829 for six assets at 8,766. This supports a bounded multiasset compute improvement, with essentially flat single-asset timing. It measured neither new canonical scores nor all-work parity, and does not close the selected FAST 80-panel score deficit. These ratios are per-context paired results, not a global runtime guarantee or website/CTV result.

The already-run stochastic and dependence branches do not give a speed-qualified route to the strict reference: the fixed original-node IID case is substantially slower than selected FAST; the rough Markov case measured about 2.10x and 1.98x selected-FAST time in the saved single/multi contexts; and the variance-only IID branch was also parked after paired speed trials exceeded FAST. These are branch-specific measurements, not claims that every possible implementation is slow. They make another expensive composition without a newly identified cost or score mechanism a poor next use of the canonical panels.

The rough-MAP optimization idea remains a source-level feasibility possibility, not a remaining scoring law. In one paired SPY context the recorded rough worker was 43.69 ms within a 110.30 ms candidate versus 55.24 ms selected FAST. Removing the **entire** worker arithmetically leaves 66.61 ms (1.206x FAST); an exact gradient affects only part of that worker. This single case rules out neither combined implementation work nor every other context, but it does not support the gradient alone as a strict-FAST path. Any changed fitted MAP would require fresh score validation.

## Unevaluated ideas and limits

The current-FAST-marginals × static Student copula cell was not found among the scored rows. The closest saved prior-law comparisons favor dynamic dependence over M251 static Student (M255/M256 aggregate scores are lower), but those prior rows use different asset marginals and do not answer the exact current-law cell. The current profile identifies cDCC as a meaningful multiasset cost, while dimension-one dependence is already an identity; static dependence still requires correlated draws and probability mapping, and there is no matched current-FAST timing. Thus the cell is untested, not impossible, but the source/saved evidence does not make a new score gain credible enough to justify the timing and panel spend. A current-training held-out dependence diagnostic that specifically implicates cDCC dynamics would change this assessment.

Likewise, finite-difference rough fitting has a real measured cost, but no exact-gradient timing or equivalent fit has been established. A full-worker removal cannot meet FAST in the cited single case; a gradient would remove only a fraction. Treat this as an engineering feasibility branch only if exact objective/fit preservation can first be shown, not as evidence for a new forecast law.

## Decision boundary

On current evidence, retain the bounded native cDCC optimization as promising implementation evidence; do not label it a score winner. Do not start another full-panel candidate solely because an interaction is unscored. Reopen candidate expansion only if a saved, current-law diagnostic identifies a specific remaining mechanism and a quick paired strict-FAST screen shows the implementation can meet the runtime contract. A first joint-score win alone still does not satisfy the user's stopping rule: complete both canonical panels, matched timing, independent/publication checks, long-growth assessment, and archival/cleanup requirements before selecting or closing.
