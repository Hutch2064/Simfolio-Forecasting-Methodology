# Post-total-mean remaining-mechanism and stopping assessment — 2026-10-19

Read-only assessment of saved scores, ledgers, and diagnostic records. No model imports, forecasts, financial calls, native builds, workers, candidate edits, or changes to frozen evidence were made. This is a dated addendum; the existing stopping ledger and 08:30 mechanism scan remain preserved and unchanged.

## Finding

The full conditional-total-mean law is complete and independently audited, but it still does not beat M261 on both panels. On 52, CRPS is `0.5973358294`: 2.6934% below M261 (`0.6138695811`) but 0.2625% above selected RMS with corrected nodes (`0.5957716608`). On 80, it is `0.2315534441`: 2.1305% above M261 (`0.2267230885`) but 1.2864% below selected (`0.2345710242`). Relative to total covariance plus the earlier conventional-only mean term, adding the normalized rough first-moment term improves 80 by only 0.0684% and worsens 52 by 0.0566%. No joint winner is established.

This result closes the previously pending question about the fixed `b*(dc*dr-1)` first-moment adjustment under the normalized conditional-logscale approximation. It does not restore M261's original `sqrt(kappa)` marginal scale, future rough-state persistence, or full mapped distribution. Nor does its score identify the first-moment shift as the cause of the difference: clipping, covariance emission, rebalancing, and compounding remain in the scored law.

The independent publication seal passes. It reconstructs the saved CRPS cells, counts, task identities, and controls byte-exactly for all 2,652 panel-52 origins and 4,080 panel-80 origins at 240 paths, with no failures. Its scope is saved-score/summary verification; complete-path hashes and same-path long-growth metrics are execution/source-bound because full path arrays were not retained. This is a strong audit of the reported panel scores, not an independent forecast rerun or post-search validation.

## Portfolio and horizon pattern

The asset/portfolio results remain heterogeneous. The total-mean law has lower aggregate CRPS than M261 for 24 of 52 single-asset entries and 31 of 80 portfolios; it improves on selected RMS for 30 of 52 and 55 of 80, respectively. These are counts of saved per-entry aggregate scores, not probabilities of winning on a new asset or portfolio.

In the nine saved horizon bands, panel 52's total-mean score is better than M261 in every band, but worse than selected RMS in every band. For panel 80, it is better than M261 over 1–504 days, worse over 505–2,520 days, better over 2,521–5,040 days, and worse again over 5,041–8,766 days. The last band is 4.53% worse than M261 even though the total-mean law improves on selected RMS there by 1.61%. Against selected, the panel-80 law is slightly worse through 504 days and better in the four longer bands. These are descriptive reductions of the same reused panels; they do not establish a horizon-specific mechanism or justify horizon routing.

The saved long-growth records cover 2,922-, 5,844-, and 8,766-day contexts. Among records with positive training-period CAGR, the median ratios of forecast path-median CAGR / path-mean CAGR to training CAGR are:

At 8,766 days, 37/80 panel-80 median-path CAGRs and 40/80 mean-path CAGRs exceed training CAGR; none of the 46 positive-history panel-52 records do. For the distinct `CAGR_of_mean_terminal_wealth` statistic, the median ratio to training CAGR is 0.836 on 52 and 1.127 on 80 at that horizon, illustrating why it must not be conflated with mean- or median-path CAGR. The 80 observations are portfolio contexts; these summaries are descriptive, not a claim that mean compensation caused historical convergence or improved realized returns. The audit did not independently regenerate the full paths underlying these metrics.

## Remaining mechanism and stopping rule

The IID conditional-scale law is the sole remaining bounded contrast in the reviewed evidence. Its source/pure review is marked PASS (633 source pins, 590 resource pins, 15 pure tests), but the actual six-case numerical pilot is explicitly **prepared, not run**. The pilot record still requires its scoped independent review and parent resource/admission preflight; a pilot alone would not establish either full-panel score or robustness. If admitted, passes its gates, and is independently reviewed, one fixed law may proceed to both complete panels. It must remain an alternative law, not be stacked with the deterministic total-mean correction: ideally the random multiplier and `b*(dc*dr-1)` produce the same preclip mean, so adding both double-counts that mean adjustment. It is also not a pure serial-clustering isolation because the IID factors change cross-asset dependence.

After that one IID contrast, no further interaction is supported by the present diagnostics. The retained-rough/no-conventional reserve still uses original nodes and loses M261 on 80; corrected-node × retained-rough has no scored cell or observed interaction. Adding the conventional-only mean term to that reserve is coherent but unscored, and the modest gain from the total-mean term under analytic total covariance does not transport to a simulated rough-path law. The node-by-conventional interaction was measured under rough-off conditions and does not establish a node-by-rough interaction. The stopping ledger's older untested EWMA-anchor and direct-Student terminal-variance choices likewise have no current-RMS diagnostic supporting a new cross. If the IID law misses either full-panel M261 score, stop this local mechanism branch; if it beats both, stop method search and proceed to verification. Do not add a node, reserve, mean, seed, count, or horizon grid.

## Minimum evidence for a final recommendation

1. Complete the IID pilot's source review and parent resource/preflight gates, then independently review the actual pilot results. The six fixed 240-path cases are an admission check only, not full-panel evidence.
2. If admitted, score the frozen law on the full canonical 52 and 80 panels against identical M261 and selected controls at 240 paths, then independently audit task identities, all score cells/counts, and paired results. A two-panel research winner requires a lower aggregate CRPS than M261 on **both** panels.
3. Predeclare at least one independent-seed full-panel sensitivity replication for the finalist and M261 on identical tasks. One replication is only a seed-sensitivity check; a robustness claim needs multiple paired seeds and seed-level uncertainty, with no seed selection.
4. Measure the finalist against selected and M261 in the existing six-context matched fresh/cached protocol (three fresh processes per model/context, one fresh fit plus three cached-fit samples), including single-asset and actual multiasset cases and stage-separated timings. Selected's prior local medians of 2.8646× fresh and 4.1387× cached versus M261 do not transfer to IID. Report the measured tradeoff without inventing a numeric speed threshold; these are local compute timings, not deployed click-to-visible evidence.
5. Because both canonical panels have been used for methodology search, describe any result as a bounded panel comparison. A general predictive-superiority claim needs a new, frozen prospective/chronological holdout that is not used to choose among laws. A source review or six-case integration pilot is not that validation.
